The 30-second pitch (what PolySim actually does)

On Polymarket, people bet real money on whether things will happen — "Will OpenAI release a model by May?", "Will Autodesk beat earnings?", etc. Every bet is public on a blockchain. Sometimes, the people betting have inside information — they know something the rest of the market doesn't.

PolySim's job: watch every trade live, profile the wallets making them, and flag the ones whose trading pattern looks like an insider. When it flags one, it simulates copying their bet with fake money — and then, months later, measures whether copying insiders actually makes money.

It never places a real trade. The operator (you) can choose to act on what the system shows, but the software itself is strictly read-only on market data and strictly paper-trading on the execution side. That separation is non-negotiable.

How the system flows (left to right)

1. IngestWatch Polymarket's live trade stream, save every trade to a local database.
2. ProfileFor each wallet, track: age, funding source, categories traded, win rate.
3. Score (detectors)Five rules each score "how insider-like is this?" on a 0-1 scale.
4. ComposeCombine the five scores into one 0-10 composite. If > 5, we call it a flag.
5. Investigate (AI)Claude reads the evidence and judges INFORMED vs LUCKY vs UNCLEAR.
6. Paper-tradeIf INFORMED, open a simulated copy bet, track it to resolution.
7. ReportAfter N days, did this strategy beat baselines? Produce the verdict.

Jargon decoder (read once, keep nearby)

Polymarket
A prediction-market exchange where YES/NO shares in real-world outcomes trade at a price between 0¢ and 100¢. Price ≈ market's estimated probability. Winning shares pay $1, losing pay $0.
Wallet / address
A blockchain account (0xaf4…0011). Every trader has one. A "fresh" wallet is brand-new.
Nonce
How many transactions a wallet has ever made. nonce=3 means only 3 transactions ever — almost certainly a brand-new account.
YES / NO share
A bet that something will / won't happen. Buying YES at 32¢ means risking $0.32 to win $1 if YES occurs.
Mid price
Midpoint between the best buy offer and the best sell offer. Roughly the market consensus.
Contrarian bet
Betting against the current consensus. "Contrarian +10pts" means buying at 32¢ when the market mid was 22¢.
Niche market
A market with thin daily trading volume (e.g. < $500K/day). Thin = where insiders are easiest to spot and hardest to fade.
CLOB
Central Limit Order Book — the system that matches buy and sell orders. Polymarket uses one.
Funding source
Where a wallet's first deposit came from (Binance, Coinbase, unknown). Clues about the owner.
Composite score
0–10. PolySim's aggregate insider-likeness rating for one (wallet, market) combo. Threshold to flag: ≥ 5.
Detector
One of five rules that each score a specific angle: CategoryInsider (win rate in a niche), EventInsider (fresh wallet + big contrarian bet), Fresh (new wallet activity), Coordination (wallets moving together), Timing (late-bet concentration).
Investigator
Claude (the AI) reading a flag's evidence and issuing a verdict: INFORMED, LUCKY, or UNCLEAR. Filter, not scorer.
Paper trade
A simulated bet using fake money. PolySim is paper-only, forever. See spec §16.
Copy trade
Replicating someone else's trade with your own money. The core strategy PolySim tests — copy the flagged insiders, see if it pays.
Orderbook
The stack of unfilled buy and sell offers. "Walking the book" = filling a big order by eating successively worse prices.
Slippage
Paying a worse price than you wanted because your order eats through the cheapest offers. Measured in cents per share here.
Latency
Delay from event to reaction. "8.1s latency" = we opened our copy-trade 8.1 seconds after the insider's trade. Long delays lose edge.
Fill model
PolySim's simulation of what price + timing a copy-trade would realistically get. The single biggest place where paper results can lie to you, so it's deliberately pessimistic.
Resolution
When a market's outcome is decided. Winning shares pay $1, losing $0. Sometimes "INVALID" — nobody wins.
Sharpe / Sortino
Risk-adjusted return metrics. >1 is decent, >2 is strong. Sortino only counts downside volatility.
Drawdown
Peak-to-trough loss during a run. "Max drawdown -4.8%" = at worst, balance dropped 4.8% below its previous high.
Shapley attribution
Math for fairly splitting credit for P&L across multiple contributors (each detector here). Prevents "which detector mattered most?" from being a guess.
Baseline (null / favorite)
Dumb reference strategies we compete against. Null = copy random wallets at the same rate. Favorite = always buy the current market leader. Our strategy must statistically beat both, or it's not real.
Kill switch
Automatic safety cutoff. E.g., if drawdown exceeds 20% of starting balance, the system pauses itself and waits for operator confirmation.
Composite ≥ 5, ≥ 2 detectors
The rule for raising a flag: composite score of at least 5 AND at least two different detectors contributed. Prevents single-detector false alarms.

1 The Terminal — live data coming in command: polysim ingest start

What am I looking at?

A command-line terminal showing the ingestion pipeline as it runs. Every line is a real event happening on Polymarket, arriving via a persistent WebSocket connection. Think of it as a stock ticker for prediction-market bets.

Why this exists

Before you can detect anything, you need the data. This is the foundation — it listens to Polymarket's firehose, saves every trade to a local database (polysim.db), and enriches each wallet with metadata from the blockchain.

How to read the lines
  • Green lines with → at the top: system status (connection established, services started).
  • [14:32:11] TRADE lines: one trade each. 0x8af…c2a1 is the wallet; YES@63¢ means they bought YES at 63 cents; $150 is the trade size.
  • Red "↑" callouts: the system noticing something worth attention ("fresh wallet", "contrarian bet"). These are hints, not flags yet.
  • Yellow FLAG #1842: the moment something crosses threshold — the scorer fires, Claude investigates, and if INFORMED, a paper position opens.
  • Purple NEW_MARKET: a new Polymarket market just went live; we classified it into a category.
  • Purple RESOLVE: a market just paid out; our paper position closed.
The story in this capture

Watch wallet 0xaf4…0011: at 14:32:22 it makes a big first trade. 22 seconds later, it places a second big trade on an unrelated AI market, betting against consensus. That second trade tips its composite score over 5 → Claude reviews → INFORMED → a $50 paper position opens. That entire decision pipeline took ~4 seconds.

What you'd do here

Normally, leave it running in a terminal and forget about it. Glance at the heartbeat line (bottom) to confirm data is still flowing. Real monitoring happens in panel 2.

2 The Dashboard — everything at a glance command: polysim dashboard

What am I looking at?

A full-screen text dashboard that updates in place, not by scrolling. Like Task Manager, but for your insider-detection strategy. Think of this as mission control — everything important is visible on one screen.

Why this exists

Panel 1's terminal is a firehose. You can't watch it all day. The dashboard aggregates and summarizes: what flags fired recently, what positions are open, is the strategy making money, are any safety switches close to tripping? One glance tells you the state of the system.

Walking through the six boxes
  • LIVE FLAGS (top-left): the last 11 flags, with score and verdict. "INFORMED 8.4" = Claude said insider, composite 8.4/10.
  • OPEN POSITIONS (top-right): simulated bets currently open. Green = winning on paper, red = losing. "Claude 5 Q3 YES +$13.50" = we bought YES on the Claude 5 market, currently up $13.50.
  • P&L BY CATEGORY (middle-left): profit by niche. ai is the breadwinner today (+$54). pop_culture is losing — operator should reconsider keeping it enabled.
  • KILL SWITCHES (middle-right, top): live safety status. Drawdown 0.9% vs 20% limit = plenty of headroom. Investigator 12/100 = we've used 12 of today's 100 Claude calls.
  • SOURCE WALLETS (middle-right, bottom): which traders we're currently "following." Exposure is capped at 10% per wallet — 0xaf4…0011 at 4.2% is healthy.
  • BASELINE COMPARISON (bottom-right): the honest-accountant panel. p=0.031 ✓ means we're beating random-wallet-copying at 96.9% statistical confidence. Without this, we'd never know if our edge is real.
Keyboard shortcuts (bottom bar)

F + a flag number pops up the flag detail (that's panel 3). P does the same for positions, W for wallets. R refreshes, Q quits.

What you'd do here

Check in a couple of times a day. If a number looks weird, drill in with the keyboard shortcuts. If kill switches are yellow/red, pause the run and investigate — the spec requires manual resume after any switch trips.

3 Flag detail — "why did you flag this?" command: polysim flags show 1842

What am I looking at?

A deep dive on a single flag. When the system says "wallet X is suspicious," this view shows everything it knows: who the wallet is, what they bet on, which detectors contributed to the score, and the AI investigator's case for/against the INFORMED verdict.

Why this exists

Auditability. Every flag must be reproducible (spec §14): given the stored inputs, running the scorer again must produce the identical flag. This view is the receipt — the operator can eyeball the evidence and decide whether the detector is right.

Section-by-section
  • Wallet block: profile of the actor. "Nonce=3, 2 days old, $12,400 lifetime, no resolved markets yet" = classic fresh account with no track record.
  • Market block: what they bet on. "$47,200 daily volume" = niche, thin market — hard to trade into without leaving fingerprints.
  • Triggering trade: the specific bet. "Contrarian +10pts" = they paid 32¢ when consensus was 22¢. That's a 45% premium over market — either informed or crazy.
  • Composite score 8.4/10: the math behind the flag.
    • Five detectors each produce a 0-1 score. We multiply by weights and sum.
    • Here: Event × 2.5 = 2.35, Coord × 1.5 = 1.23, Fresh × 1.0 = 0.98. Total = 4.56.
    • The +3.84 "stacked-signal bonus" fires when 3+ detectors all agree with high confidence — heuristic to boost scores on multi-angle evidence. Final 8.40.
  • Investigator verdict: Claude's read. Three elements:
    • RED FLAGS — reasons to believe this is insider activity.
    • GREEN FLAGS — reasons to be skeptical / alternate explanations. Critical for avoiding confirmation bias.
    • REASONING — the full argument in plain English.
  • Acted on: YES: the system decided to copy. $50 paper position opened 8.1 seconds after the insider's trade, filled at 34¢ vs the insider's 32¢ (2¢ slippage).
The key thing to notice

The detectors found the pattern mathematically (EventInsider=0.94). The investigator gave it the narrative check. Both agree → we copy. If the composite were 8.4 but Claude said LUCKY, we'd log it but not act. That's the two-stage filter in action.

4 Telegram — push alerts on your phone channel: @PolySimBot

What am I looking at?

A simulation of the Telegram chat the operator sees on their phone. When important events fire, PolySim pushes a formatted message. Four alert types, each with a color-coded left border.

Why this exists

You can't watch a terminal all day. Telegram lets PolySim tap you on the shoulder only when it matters. Flag fired? Position resolved for a big win? Daily summary ready? Your phone buzzes. Everything else stays silent.

The four alert types
  • 🚩 Flag raised (yellow border) — a new flag crossed threshold and was INFORMED by Claude. Shows the evidence, the paper position that opened.
  • 🟢 Position opened (green border) — confirmation that the simulated trade actually filled. Shows size, price, current exposure.
  • 🔴 Position resolved (lost) (red border) — a market decided and we lost. Shows how much and how long we held.
  • 🟩 Position resolved (won) (green border) — we won. Same details.
  • 📊 Daily summary (purple border) — 09:00 digest of yesterday: P&L, flags acted, best/worst category, API usage.
Why there's a bot at all

The system is designed to be operator-in-the-loop. It takes no action in the real world — but it tells you, promptly and in readable form, what it would have done. You can then decide (outside this system, with a clean air gap per spec §16) whether any of this warrants action elsewhere.

Settings you'd tweak

In config.yml: telegram.enabled, daily summary time, which alert types to enable, per-flag minimum composite to alert on. All optional — CLI-only is also a supported mode.

5 The Report — the verdict after a run command: polysim report --run 5 --format md

What am I looking at?

The end-of-run report card. After a paper run ends (scheduled, or when you stop it), PolySim generates a Markdown document with every metric needed to answer the project's one question: did this strategy work, or did I fool myself?

Why this exists

Success isn't "did I make money in paper." It's "did I beat the obvious baselines at statistical confidence, with realistic fill assumptions, with no hidden reliance on weird edge cases?" The report is where that gets proven — or disproven.

Reading the sections
  • Blockquote at top — the one-line verdict. PASS/FAIL against the spec's acceptance criteria.
  • 1. Headline metrics — total P&L, Sharpe (risk-adjusted return), Sortino (downside-only version), drawdown, win rate. If Sharpe < 1 or max drawdown > 10%, dig deeper.
  • 2. P&L by category — per-niche breakdown. You learn here which categories actually have edge vs which just add noise. (Spoiler: in this sample, pop_culture lost money. Real-run data would drive a decision to disable it.)
  • 3. Detector attribution (Shapley) — fair credit-assignment across the five detectors. "Event is 42% of P&L" tells you which rules are pulling their weight. Under-performing detectors become refactor targets.
  • 4. Baseline comparisons — the honesty section. Null (copy random wallets) lost money, Favorite (always buy consensus) made a little. Primary beat both with p < 0.05, meaning <5% chance the outperformance was luck. Without this, P&L alone is meaningless.
  • 5. Top source wallets — which insiders we copied profitably. Top contributor gets pinned into known_insiders[] for faster flagging next run.
  • 6. Execution drag — how much edge we lost to latency and slippage vs a perfect (impossible) counterfactual that filled at the insider's exact price. 11% drag on ai means we captured 89% of theoretical edge. This is also the most important table for keeping yourself honest — low drag means your fill model might be too optimistic.
  • 7. Calibration plot — ASCII bar chart. Does higher composite score actually correspond to higher hit rate? Should climb monotonically. Flatness at the top = overconfidence at high scores, something to tune.
  • 8. Invalid markets — markets that resolved "INVALID" (nobody wins). If >10% of closes land here, the strategy is suspect.
  • 9. Kill-switch activity — did any safety mechanism fire? What happened?
  • 10. Anomalies & notes — free-form observations. Usually the actionable next-steps list.
What you'd do here

Two things. (1) Read the blockquote + §4 first — did we pass? (2) If yes, read §2 and §6 to decide what to tune before the next run. If no, read the full thing and figure out why.

How this gets delivered

Produced on demand by polysim report --run N, also auto-written to ./reports/ when a run stops. Can be opened in any Markdown viewer, GitHub, or the terminal via glow/mdcat.

Wrap-up — putting it all together

You've just seen, in order: raw data coming in (1), a compact summary of everything (2), the deep-dive when something suspicious happens (3), the phone buzz that tells you about it (4), and the final verdict after a month of operation (5). Those five surfaces are the entire user-facing footprint of PolySim — there is no web UI, no hosted version, no multi-user system. One operator, one laptop, one database file.

The whole thing is read-only on the outside world: it looks at public Polymarket data, it looks at public blockchain data, it calls Claude for analysis. It never signs a transaction or places an order. If you, the operator, ever decide to act on what it shows — that happens outside this system, in your own head and your own hands. That air gap is load-bearing and intentional.
